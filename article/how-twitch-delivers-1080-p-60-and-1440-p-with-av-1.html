How Twitch Delivers 1080p60 and 1440p with AV1

Twitch leverages the advanced compression efficiency of the AV1 codec to overcome the bitrate limitations of legacy H.264 streaming, enabling creators to broadcast high-motion gaming content in crystal-clear 1080p60 and 1440p. By integrating AV1 into its Enhanced Broadcasting framework alongside dedicated hardware encoding from modern GPUs, Twitch significantly reduces bandwidth demands while maximizing video fidelity and viewer accessibility across diverse network conditions.

Overcoming H.264 Bitrate Limitations

For over a decade, Twitch’s live streaming infrastructure relied almost entirely on the AVC/H.264 codec, capped at a soft ingest limit of roughly 6,000 to 8,000 kbps. While sufficient for slow-paced content, this bandwidth envelope causes severe macroblocking, pixelation, and motion blur during fast-paced gameplay such as first-person shooters or racing titles at 1080p60. Delivering 1440p (2K) under these constraints was virtually impossible without degrading stream stability or alienating viewers on constrained mobile connections.

AV1 solves this bottleneck by providing roughly 30% to 50% better compression efficiency compared to H.264. This allows Twitch to transmit the same visual quality at nearly half the bitrate, or dramatically increase resolution and fidelity at standard ingest speeds.

Client-Side Multi-Track Encoding via Enhanced Broadcasting

Live video transcoding at Twitch’s scale is computationally expensive. Traditional pipelines required streamers to send a single high-bitrate video feed, which Twitch servers then decoded and re-encoded into multiple lower resolutions (transcodes) for viewers.

To deploy AV1 efficiently, Twitch introduced Enhanced Broadcasting in collaboration with companies like NVIDIA, AMD, and Intel, as well as software developers like OBS Studio. This architecture offloads multi-resolution encoding to the streamer’s local hardware:

  • Hardware AV1 Encoders: Modern GPUs (such as NVIDIA GeForce RTX 40-Series, AMD Radeon RX 7000-Series, and Intel Arc) feature dedicated silicon for hardware-accelerated AV1 encoding, executing compression with negligible impact on in-game performance.
  • Simultaneous Multi-Encode: Instead of transmitting just one feed, the streamer's client encodes and pushes multiple resolutions and codecs simultaneously (e.g., an AV1 track for 1440p or 1080p, and fallback H.264 tracks for lower resolutions).
  • Reduced Server Burden: Because the creator's machine generates the different stream qualities directly, Twitch’s edge ingest servers act primarily as a distribution and routing layer rather than consuming massive compute resources to transcode high-density streams in real time.

High-Density 1440p and Artifact-Free 1080p60

High-density gaming broadcasts benefit directly from AV1’s advanced coding tools, such as larger block sizes (Superblocks up to 128x128), directional intra-prediction, and sophisticated motion compensation.

  1. Elimination of Motion Artifacts at 1080p60: In high-complexity scenes—such as foliage in open-world games or particle effects in shooters—AV1 preserves sharp edges and fine details without the bitrate spikes that cause frame drops in older codecs.
  2. Accessible 1440p Streams: 1440p requires more than 1.7 times the pixel count of standard 1080p. AV1 allows broadcast of this resolution within an accessible 8,000 to 10,000 kbps range, fitting comfortably within typical domestic broadband upload capacities.

Dynamic Delivery and Backward Compatibility

While modern desktops, smart TVs, and mobile devices increasingly feature native AV1 decoding hardware, a substantial portion of Twitch's global audience still uses legacy hardware that relies on software decoding, which can drain batteries or cause dropped frames.

Twitch handles this through hybrid manifest generation. When an AV1-compatible broadcast is ingested:

  • Devices with AV1 hardware or capable software decoders receive the primary AV1 stream at high resolutions (1440p or pristine 1080p60).
  • Devices lacking AV1 support automatically receive a secondary H.264 or HEVC stream supplied by the Enhanced Broadcasting multi-track pipeline.

This hybrid edge delivery ensures that adopting high-density AV1 feeds does not fracture Twitch's user base, providing maximum quality to viewers with modern devices while maintaining seamless backward compatibility for everyone else.
