How AOMedia Defensive Patent License Protects AV1

The Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) designed the AV1 video codec to deliver high-performance, next-generation video compression without the encumbrance of costly and unpredictable licensing fees. Central to this mission is the AOMedia Patent License 1.0, a defensive licensing framework that actively protects AV1 implementers, distributors, and hardware manufacturers from patent infringement lawsuits. This article explains how the license operates, detailing its royalty-free grants, defensive termination provisions, non-assertion covenants, and collective legal safeguards that shield the AV1 ecosystem from litigation.

Universal, Royalty-Free Patent Grants

Under the AOMedia Patent License, every participating member—including tech industry leaders such as Google, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, Intel, and Netflix—grants a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive license for their essential patent claims to anyone implementing AV1.

This grant covers any patent claim owned or controlled by a member that is unavoidably infringed by practicing the AV1 specification. By providing these rights free of charge and without recurring royalty obligations, the license removes the baseline legal risk typically associated with adopting complex multimedia standards. Implementers can build hardware decoders, author encoding software, or stream AV1 content without negotiating individual bilateral licenses with member companies.

Defensive Termination Provisions

The strongest legal shield within the AOMedia framework is its defensive termination clause. This clause creates a powerful deterrent against patent aggression by establishing that an entity's own license to AV1 essential patents is revoked if they initiate patent litigation.

Specifically, if an implementer or patent holder files a patent infringement claim against another party alleging that an AV1-compliant implementation infringes their patents, the aggressor's license to all AOMedia members' AV1 patents terminates automatically. Because the AOMedia consortium controls a massive portfolio of foundational digital video patents, losing rights to this collective intellectual property exposes the aggressor to devastating counterclaims. This structure creates a "patent peace" equilibrium, making it economically and legally perilous for any entity utilizing AV1 to sue other AV1 implementers.

Broad Reciprocal Non-Assertion

AOMedia members agree to a strict covenant not to sue (non-assertion pledge) regarding the AV1 specification. By binding all contributors to this standard, the license effectively creates a patent-cleared commons among participating organizations.

Implementers who do not contribute directly still benefit from this reciprocal architecture. As long as an implementer does not assert its own patents against AV1 implementations, it maintains uninhibited, perpetual rights to use the entire pool of essential patents held by the alliance.

Collective Defense Against Third-Party Claims

While the AOMedia license binds participating members, non-member entities—such as patent pools and non-practicing entities (patent trolls)—may still attempt to assert external patents against AV1. To mitigate this risk, AOMedia established an internal legal infrastructure and defense fund.

When third-party patent pools announce licensing claims against AV1, AOMedia actively reviews the asserted claims to locate prior art, contest patent validity, and provide collective legal assistance to affected members and downstream implementers. Furthermore, the extensive patent portfolios of AOMedia’s founding members provide immense counter-assertion leverage against aggressive outside patent owners who rely on standard web, hardware, or cloud technologies produced by the consortium.

Through the combination of unconditional royalty-free grants, strict termination penalties for litigants, reciprocal covenants, and collective industry backing, the AOMedia defensive patent license establishes a robust, self-enforcing safe harbor for anyone implementing the AV1 codec.
