AV1 Support in Premiere Pro and Media Frameworks

The AV1 video codec offers royalty-free, high-efficiency compression designed to succeed HEVC (H.265) and AVC (H.264), but its adoption differs significantly across professional software and open ecosystems. While open-source media frameworks have fully embraced both software and hardware implementations of AV1 for recording, transcoding, and streaming, commercial non-linear editors like Adobe Premiere Pro have taken a slower, phased approach to native integration. This article examines the current state of AV1 support in Adobe Premiere Pro alongside leading open-source frameworks like FFmpeg, GStreamer, and OBS Studio.

AV1 Support in Adobe Premiere Pro

Adobe Premiere Pro's native AV1 implementation focuses primarily on playback and decoding rather than full-pipeline authoring:

  • Hardware Decoding: Premiere Pro supports hardware-accelerated AV1 decoding on supported systems, such as Intel 11th-generation processors and newer, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 30-series and newer, AMD Radeon RX 7000-series, and Apple M3-series chips. This allows editors to import and edit AV1 footage natively without mandatory pre-transcoding.
  • Software Decoding: Systems lacking dedicated hardware decoders may experience sluggish timeline playback, as software decoding of AV1 is computationally demanding inside a timeline environment.
  • Exporting and Encoding: Native AV1 export options remain largely absent or limited in the default Adobe Media Encoder presets. Editors who need to output directly to AV1 from Premiere Pro typically rely on third-party export plugins, most notably Voukoder, which acts as a bridge to open-source encoding libraries.

AV1 in Open Media Frameworks

Open media frameworks lead the industry in AV1 implementation, driven directly by members and contributors of the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia).

  • FFmpeg: FFmpeg provides comprehensive, production-grade support for AV1. It integrates dav1d, widely recognized as the fastest software AV1 decoder available. For encoding, FFmpeg supports SVT-AV1 (Scalable Video Technology for AV1), which offers an optimal balance between compression efficiency and speed, alongside hardware-accelerated encoders such as av1_nvenc (NVIDIA), av1_qsv (Intel), and av1_amf (AMD).
  • OBS Studio: OBS provides native AV1 recording and live streaming. Users with modern GPUs from NVIDIA, AMD, or Intel can capture content directly in AV1 using hardware encoders. OBS also supports software encoding via SVT-AV1 for streaming to compatible platforms like YouTube.
  • GStreamer: GStreamer includes robust plugins for AV1 parsing, decoding, and muxing within media pipelines. It supports both software decoders (dav1d) and hardware interfaces (VA-API, NVCODEC), making it a standard choice for embedded systems, media servers, and Linux-based multimedia applications.

The Ecosystem Divide

The disparity between Premiere Pro and open media frameworks stems from different design priorities:

  1. Agility: Open-source projects like FFmpeg and OBS can quickly adopt emerging standards and hardware APIs without waiting for long commercial release cycles.
  2. Resource Allocation: Commercial suites prioritize timeline stability, color management pipelines, and broad hardware acceleration over early adoption of complex software encoders that can severely burden CPU resources.

Current Workflow Recommendations

For video professionals working with AV1 today:

  • In Premiere Pro: Import AV1 clips directly if your system has dedicated hardware decoding; otherwise, transcode source footage to an editing-friendly intermediate codec like ProRes or DNxHR. For exports, render to an intermediate format and use FFmpeg or HandBrake to compress the final deliverable into AV1.
  • In Open Frameworks: Use dav1d for reading files and SVT-AV1 or modern hardware encoders (NVENC/QSV/AMF) for rapid, high-quality encoding and live distribution.
