Apple M3 AV1 Support: Encoding vs Decoding

The Apple M3 processor family introduces dedicated hardware acceleration for the modern AV1 video codec, but this capability is limited strictly to decoding. While the M3, M3 Pro, and M3 Max chips deliver highly efficient, battery-friendly playback of AV1-encoded media from platforms like YouTube and Netflix, they lack a dedicated hardware block for AV1 encoding. This article breaks down the media engine architecture across the M3 lineup, detailing which codecs support hardware-accelerated creation and what these limitations mean for video editors and livestreamers.

AV1 Decoding on the M3 Chipset

With the introduction of the M3 series, Apple integrated an AV1 decode engine directly into the silicon's Media Engine. AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) is an open-source, royalty-free video format designed to provide superior compression compared to HEVC (H.265) and AVC (H.264) at the same or higher visual quality.

Because decoding is handled natively by the Media Engine rather than the general-purpose CPU or GPU cores, M3-powered Macs can stream 4K and 8K AV1 content seamlessly. This offloading significantly reduces power consumption, prevents thermal throttling, and preserves battery life during video streaming.

Lack of Hardware-Accelerated AV1 Encoding

Despite having dedicated silicon for playback, the Apple M3 family does not feature a hardware AV1 encoder. The Media Engine on the M3, M3 Pro, and M3 Max continues to restrict hardware encoding to the following standard formats:

  • H.264 (AVC)
  • H.265 (HEVC)
  • Apple ProRes and ProRes RAW

Users can still export video in the AV1 format using applications such as HandBrake, FFmpeg, or compatible non-linear editors, but the task must be processed entirely via software encoding using CPU threads. While the high-performance CPU cores of the M3 series can complete these tasks, software encoding is considerably slower, generates more heat, and drains battery life much faster than dedicated hardware encoding.

Practical Implications for Users

For the general consumer and media viewer, the hardware AV1 decoder covers nearly every everyday use case, ensuring future-proof web browsing and video streaming across modern video platforms transitioning away from HEVC and VP9.

For video professionals, livestreamers, and digital creators looking to produce AV1 content directly, the absence of an AV1 hardware encoder remains a limitation compared to modern competitors. Discrete GPUs such as NVIDIA's RTX 40-series, AMD's Radeon RX 7000-series, and Intel's Arc graphics cards feature dual hardware AV1 decode and encode blocks. Content creators using the M3 family will need to rely on HEVC or ProRes for hardware-accelerated exports, or accept the performance overhead of software-based AV1 rendering.
