When Was the AV1 Bitstream Specification Released?

This article provides a concise overview of the AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) video codec, focusing on its official standardization timeline and development milestones. It specifically highlights the exact year the first frozen bitstream specification was finalized, the significance of a frozen bitstream for hardware and software adoption, and the subsequent impact of AV1 on modern internet video streaming.

The Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) officially released the first frozen bitstream specification for the AV1 codec in 2018 (specifically on March 28, 2018). The official public release of the specification, alongside reference code and licensing details, followed later that year in June.

A "frozen" bitstream specification is a critical milestone in video codec development. Prior to freezing, the technical definitions and mathematical processes used to compress and decompress video data can change. Once frozen:

  • Hardware Development Can Begin: Silicon manufacturers such as Intel, AMD, Nvidia, and ARM can design chips with dedicated hardware decoders and encoders without the risk of future changes breaking compatibility.
  • Format Stability: Software developers can write decoders and encoders knowing that any valid AV1 stream created today will decode identically in the future.
  • Cross-Platform Compatibility: Platform vendors, browsers, and streaming providers can implement playback with guaranteed interoperability.

AV1 was developed by AOMedia—a consortium that includes Google, Mozilla, Cisco, Microsoft, Amazon, Netflix, and Intel—as a royalty-free, open-source successor to VP9. It was engineered to provide superior data compression compared to proprietary alternatives like HEVC (H.265), typically delivering 20% to 30% higher compression efficiency at equivalent visual fidelity.

Following the 2018 bitstream freeze, AV1 adoption expanded rapidly across major platforms. Operating systems, web browsers, and streaming giants like YouTube and Netflix integrated AV1 delivery to reduce bandwidth usage, particularly for high-resolution 4K and 8K content. Today, hardware-accelerated AV1 decoding is widely present in modern consumer graphics cards, mobile processors, and smart TVs.
